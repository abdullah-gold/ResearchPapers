1. Introduction:

Cryptocurrency is digital money that isn't controlled by any bank or government. It exists
only online and uses cryptography (secure coding) to keep transactions safe. This means no
single company or authority decides how it's managed, giving users more control over their
own money compared to traditional banking systems.

Instead of a bank keeping track of who owns what, a public, shared record called a blockchain
does that job, verified by many computers around the world. This network of computers
works together to confirm transactions, making it very difficult to fake or alter records once
they're added. Bitcoin was the first and most famous example, introduced in 2009, but there
are thousands of others now, such as Ethereum and Litecoin, each designed for different
purposes like payments, smart contracts, or app development.

People use crypto to send money across borders quickly, invest for potential profit, or build
apps like games and decentralized platforms that don't rely on a central company. However,
its value can rise and fall a lot within a short time, making it a risky investment for beginners.
Also, if you lose access to your account or forget your private key, there's usually no way to
recover it, unlike a bank account with password reset options, so users must be extra careful
in managing their crypto wallets.

2. Research Motivation

    1. Volatility problem - Crypto currency prices change rapidly and unpredictably,
         making them harder to forecast than traditional assets like stocks. This creates a need
         for smarter prediction methods.

    2. Limits of traditional models - Older prediction methods (like linear regression) can't
         handle the complex, non-linear ups and downs in crypto prices. LSTM, a deep
         learning model, is better suited for this kind of time-series data.

    3. Impact of news and sentiment - Crypto prices often react strongly to news, tweets,
         or public opinion. Ignoring this sentiment data means missing a big part of what
         actually drives price changes.

    4. Practical value - A more reliable prediction model can help investors and traders
         make smarter decisions in a market known for sudden crashes and spikes.

    5. Academic relevance - As a CSE student, this project lets us apply real skills in
         machine learning, deep learning (LSTM), and natural language processing (sentiment
         analysis) to a current, real-world problem.

3. Problem Statement

Specific Issues:
     Sentiment analysis relies only on low-frequency, passive proxies (weekly Google
         Trends) and missing real-time social media and news sentiment

     Macroeconomic and geopolitical factors (interest rates, inflation, gold prices) are
         excluded from blockchain-fundamentals-based models

     Prediction models use structured price/volume (OHLCV) data only, ignoring
         unstructured text-based sentiment signals

     No cross-asset contagion or systemic risk modeling exists, as cryptocurrencies are
         treated independently

     Frequency-decomposition methods oversimplify price dynamics into only two
         (high/low) groups

     networks are validated on a single cryptocurrency (ADA), limiting generalizability

Solution Statement:

The proposed framework collects real-time sentiment data from social media (X/Twitter,
Reddit) and crypto news portals via their APIs, and applies AI/NLP-based sentiment analysis
to generate a high-frequency sentiment score. This sentiment score, combined with historical
price data and macro-financial factors, will feed into a machine learning-based model to
predict cryptocurrency price movements improving accuracy and responsiveness over
existing low-frequency, isolated-data approaches.

Other studies have further emphasized the importance of macroeconomic and market-wide
factors. Corbet et al. (2020) found that cryptocurrency markets respond to monetary policy
changes, while Aharon et al. (2021) and Bouri et al. (2021) reported strong relationships
between exchange rates, market contagion, and cryptocurrency returns. Hajek et al. (2023)
also showed that combining investor sentiment with ensemble machine learning improves
Bitcoin price prediction.

Despite these advances, no existing study integrates blockchain fundamentals, high-frequency
multi-source sentiment, macroeconomic indicators, and advanced forecasting techniques
within a single unified framework. This research aims to address this gap by combining
AI/NLP-based sentiment analysis with blockchain and macro-financial variables to improve
cryptocurrency price prediction and portfolio performance.

                                                                                                        3 | Page
Research Gap:
Existing studies address sentiment, blockchain fundamentals, interpretability, or frequency
decomposition largely in isolation. Guidolin and Ionta (2026) rely on low-frequency
sentiment and omit macroeconomic factors; Luo and Yin's framework ignores unstructured
sentiment and contagion effects; and Zhang et al. (2026) apply only a two-group frequency
split validated on a single asset. This project addresses these gaps by integrating real-time,
AI/NLP-derived sentiment (from social media and news APIs) with macro-financial variables
within a machine learning-based prediction framework, aiming to improve both forecasting
accuracy and generalizability across cryptocurrencies.

10. Ethical Considerations

10.1 Key Ethical Considerations

Data Usage & API Compliance - Terms of service and authorized access:

     Collect social media (X/Twitter, Reddit), news, and price data strictly through official,
         authorized APIs, respecting each platform's rate limits and terms of service.

     Avoid scraping or accessing any private, restricted, or non-public data.
     Use only publicly available posts/comments for sentiment analysis; do not attempt to

         identify or profile individual users.

Data Privacy - Handling of user-generated content:

     Anonymize or aggregate social media/news text data used for sentiment scoring; do
         not store or report identifiable usernames, profiles, or personal information.

     Limit data collection to what is necessary for sentiment analysis and price prediction
         (text content, timestamps, aggregate scores).

     Securely store collected data with access limited to the research team.

Fairness & Bias - Avoiding skewed sentiment or model bias:

     Ensure sentiment analysis models are evaluated for bias across different sources (e.g.,
         not overly weighted toward a single platform or a small group of highly active
         accounts).

     Regularly validate that sentiment scores and predictions are not systematically skewed
         by bot activity, spam, or coordinated posting.

     Test model performance across multiple cryptocurrencies to avoid conclusions biased
         toward a single asset.

Transparency & Explainability - Model clarity and limitations:

     Clearly document the sentiment scoring methodology, model architecture, features
         used, and evaluation metrics.

     Disclose the assumptions and limitations of the prediction model (e.g., not a
         guaranteed financial forecasting tool).

     Make clear that this research is for academic/analytical purposes and not intended as
         direct financial or investment advice.

Data Security - Safe handling of API keys and datasets:

                                                                                                      14 | Page
     Store API keys and credentials securely (e.g., environment variables, not hard-coded
         or publicly shared).

     Maintain secure, access-controlled storage for collected datasets and trained models.
